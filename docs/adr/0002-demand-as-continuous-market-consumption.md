# Demand as continuous market consumption, not a production ceiling

> Partially superseded by ADR 0020 (daily operating clock). Treatment no longer wakes every
> `processing_time` (24 days) but every `OPERATING_TICK_DAYS` (1 day), and the market-signal TTL is
> 1 day, not 24. The PULL passages below that depend on the 24-day cadence no longer hold. A PULL
> operator now carries unmet `attempted` forward in a **PULL Production Backlog**. That is the
> per-product budget, option (b), which the cadence-contingency note said to revisit once the loop became
> finer than the consumption tick. The operator also absorbs Market Signals while FAILED, so a missed tick
> no longer expires silently. Lost Sales still do not backlog at the market.

The original model treated national demand (100k m3/year across MDF, particle board, OSB) as a one-shot ceiling. Once cumulative production crossed the target, `get_unmet_demands()` returned zero and all processors idled for the rest of the 365-day run. After more waste types with transformation pathways were added, production hit the ceiling by about day 100, and the remaining 265 days produced nothing useful.

We replaced this with a market consumption process. It is a SimPy process that fires weekly, removes products from treatment operators' `product_to_sell` storage at a seasonally varying rate, and records fulfilled and unfulfilled consumption. The annual demand envelope is still 100k m3. The difference is that the market consumes it through the year instead of the model checking it as a cumulative ceiling.

This decision changes the whole control architecture:

- **PUSH operators** react to inventory drops caused by consumption, with no explicit notification. Storage capacity is the implicit production target. There is no separate forecast parameter.
- **PULL operators** receive explicit consumption events via KanbanManager (`source_type="market"`). These cascade upstream to collectors and generators through the existing signal infrastructure.
- **Service level** becomes `total_consumed / total_attempted`, computed from weekly consumption events. It is meaningful from day 1 instead of a binary hit or miss at year end.
- **Lost sales** are tagged by reason (`no_capability` vs `stockout`) to separate structural production gaps from operational failures.
- **Consumption is distributed** in proportion to each operator's processing capacity (set as `market_share` at construction), uniformly across all product types. Operators that cannot produce a product type record structural lost sales, which exposes regional capacity gaps that matter for policy.

## Considered options

- **Time-proportional demand**: `unmet = (target * fraction_of_year) - consumed`. Rejected because it conflates demand arrival with production triggering, assumes linearity that makes a seasonal extension hard, and has edge cases (the formula goes negative if the system runs ahead) with no clean operational meaning.
- **Per-product market share**: distribute consumption only for products an operator can make. Rejected because it hides structural gaps that matter for circular economy analysis. A region with no MDF capability has real unmet demand, which points to cross-regional transport or capacity investment.
- **Backlog instead of lost sales**: unfulfilled consumption carries over. Rejected for now. Lost sales is more realistic for commodity wood products, since buyers don't wait, and it makes the service level metric harsher and better at separating strategies. Backlog is noted as a future research option.

## Consequences

- `_distribute_demand()` in SimulationManager is deleted and replaced by a `market_share` parameter on each operator, set at construction.
- `SimulationState.get_unmet_demands()`, `check_all_demands_met()` and `demand_met_times` are all deleted.
- `processor.demand` as a persistent attribute is deleted. Nothing sets it at init or reads it in a way that matters.
- `_apply_stock_strategy_to_demand_calculation()` is deleted or refactored to work purely on inventory levels.
- The `product_storage` attribute on TreatmentOperator is deleted. Under the old ceiling model it was a write-only overflow sink. Production beyond `product_to_sell` capacity landed in `product_storage` with no drain path (and `product_to_sell` itself was rarely drained, because `fulfill_demand` subtracted at production time). Once market consumption drains the finished-goods inventory continuously, a secondary buffer makes no sense. Mass balance simplifies to `products_made = products_consumed + finished_goods + production_discarded`. The monitoring layer (`waste_monitor.py: product_storage_by_type`) and the visualization layer (`storage_visualization.py`) drop the secondary buffer track. Figure 14 (product-storage heatmap) disappears from the results, and the methodology section should state the structural simplification instead of leaving it unexplained.
- The `product_to_sell` attribute and `product_to_sell_capacity` parameter are renamed to `finished_goods` and `finished_goods_capacity` across `TreatmentOperator` (`core/treatment.py`, which now also holds the former `core/treatment_utils.py` logic after the Candidate 1 inlining), `models/entities.py`, `core/facility_builder.py`, `monitoring/waste_monitor.py` and `monitoring/visualization/storage_visualization.py`. The old name only made sense in contrast to the now-deleted `product_storage`. With one inventory left, the suffix misleads. The new name matches standard SCM terminology. `waste_storage` is effectively raw materials, and `finished_goods` completes the raw-materials-to-finished-goods pair used in the inventory literature and in the Tako & Robinson SCM-DES vocabulary of the methodological framing. The rename happens in the same pass as the `product_storage` deletion to limit churn.
- PUSH operators produce up to the remaining `finished_goods` headroom, per output type. For each transformation, `amount_to_process = min(waste_storage[input], processing_capacity, headroom / efficiency)` where `headroom = finished_goods_capacity[output_type] - finished_goods[output_type]`. The clamp is per output type, so a transformation producing OSB is throttled when OSB inventory is full even if MDF has headroom. When an output has zero headroom, that transformation is skipped for the cycle while others may still run. No product is silently discarded and no headroom sits idle. Backpressure stays local. Waste keeps accumulating, and if `waste_storage` overflows, the existing landfill mechanism (`handle_storage_event` then `WasteMonitor`) takes over. PUSH does not send "inventory full" upstream as a signal, because that would be PULL semantics. Mass balance is `products_made = products_consumed + finished_goods`, with `production_discarded = 0` by construction. Rejected: full-skip semantics. It would strand small headroom for full weekly ticks. The existing `min(waste_storage, processing_capacity)` clamp is already non-atomic, so a headroom clamp is the same kind of constraint and does not break atomicity.
- `finished_goods_capacity` is **per product type**: `finished_goods_capacity[P] = market_share × (annual_demand[P] / 52) × 4` (4 weeks of that product's expected consumption, no safety inflation). The aggregate buffer is their sum. It is per product, not an aggregate scalar, because the partial-batch headroom clamp throttles each output independently. MDF headroom must not hide a full OSB buffer, and an aggregate ceiling would let an ABC-favoured product take the whole buffer. `ProductStorage.capacity` becomes a per-`OutputType` dict. Initial inventory at simulation start is 2 weeks per product (50% of that product's capacity), which leaves equal headroom for growth and depletion. Capacity is a candidate for sensitivity analysis, with Monte Carlo over `weeks_of_buffer ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8}` to test whether the PUSH/PULL contrast is stable. If it is, that supports a robustness claim. If not, it is important context for interpreting the results.
- Waste-storage priming (Phase C). An operator's `waste_storage` starts at `(annual_demand_total / 52) × 2 × market_share / blended_efficiency` waste m³ (two weeks of producible throughput). It is split across the operator's accepted input waste types in proportion to the region's waste generation rates: `prime[wt] = total × gen_rate[wt] / sum(gen_rate over input types)`. A type the region does not generate gets zero (it arrives later via collection). If the region generates none of the operator's input types (the sum is zero), the split falls back to even, to avoid dividing by zero. This was chosen over an even split, which stocks locally scarce types as heavily as abundant ones, and over per-product back-calculation through the "most-efficient" transformation, which needs an arbitrary tiebreak among pathways and entangles priming with ABC. The split only matters during warm-up, since collection corrects the mix within about 2 weeks, so a realistic starting shape matters more than precision.
- Stock Strategy gates are waste-side only and do not gate `finished_goods` production. In PUSH, production triggers when the operator polls its own waste state against the strategy threshold (as before). In PULL, production responds to every consumption event with the event volume (subject to the partial-batch clamp), and the strategy gates only the upstream waste-side signaling that fires when `waste_storage` drops below the threshold. This matches thesis Sections 5.3 and 5.4.3 ("proactive ordering logic, monitoring raw material inventory levels"). The strategies are `(s, S)`-style replenishment policies on raw materials, not finished-goods buffer targets. PUSH and PULL stay distinct because the same strategy parameter drives different triggers (state polling vs event reaction). Rejected: Option A (strategy targets the `finished_goods` level) and Option C (gates both downstream and upstream), for four reasons. (a) The thesis has no finished-goods buffer-target semantics. (b) Under a uniform 50% initial inventory the ON_DEMAND vs REORDER_50 contrast becomes degenerate at steady state. (c) The chosen option extends the existing waste-side strategy gates instead of inventing a new finished-goods mechanism. (d) The bullwhip measurement (`var(orders)/var(consumption)` at each echelon, P2 in the metrics roadmap) maps cleanly onto `(s, S)` lot-for-lot vs threshold-triggered ordering. ON_DEMAND yields the textbook unit bullwhip ratio lower bound, and REORDER_X yields amplified variance.
- The current PULL `_continuous_demand_signaling` (treatment.py:350-365) uses a fixed 10% threshold for upstream signaling and ignores the stock strategy. The refactor routes the strategy through this path. PULL-ON_DEMAND signals upstream every cycle that consumed waste, and PULL-REORDER_X signals when waste_storage crosses the X% threshold. This matches PULL behavior to the PUSH `_should_trigger_collection_based_on_strategy` semantics.
- The PULL consumption-event signal carries the `attempted` volume (full demand including the unfulfilled portion), not the `consumed` volume. The PULL operator's production target is `attempted`, clamped by the partial-batch headroom on `finished_goods` and by waste and efficiency limits. This makes PULL demand-aware. An emptied `finished_goods` does not start a death spiral, because the next tick's attempted volume keeps driving production. For bullwhip, PULL-ON_DEMAND yields `var(production_orders) = var(attempted_demand)`, a unit bullwhip ratio at the treatment echelon (the textbook lower bound for lot-for-lot ordering). Defense against reviewers: the demand-visible signal is the simulation analog of real-world JIT additions (CONWIP, minimum-WIP, periodic state reviews per Hopp & Spearman *Factory Physics*). Pure stateless kanban is a textbook abstraction, not a real production policy. Lost-sale visibility stays at the SimulationState/market level for reporting and is not used for operator production decisions. Rejected: signal = consumed (death-spiral artifacts under spike scenarios, and a non-linear variance distortion that muddies the bullwhip reading), and signal = (attempted, consumed) tuple (a payload nothing else uses).
- Signal-volume rule: `S = waste_storage_capacity` (order up to full capacity), applied the same way across PUSH and PULL and all three strategy variants. When `waste_storage` drops below the threshold `s`, the upstream signal carries `signal_volume = waste_storage_capacity - current_waste_storage`. ON_DEMAND is lot-for-lot by construction (s = S = capacity, so signal_volume = amount-just-consumed). REORDER_X uses lot sizes proportional to `(1 - X) × capacity` per cycle, the textbook `(s, S)` formulation per Silver/Pyke/Peterson. This changes the current PUSH `_get_reorder_quantity` behavior (lines 288-294 use S = threshold, which gives tight oscillation), so baseline figures will change. The `on_demand_buffer_ratio` and `on_demand_target_ratio` constants in `config/constants.py` become dead code. For bullwhip, REORDER_X under S = capacity creates measurable variance amplification at each echelon (the lot-sizing effect), while ON_DEMAND yields the textbook unit bullwhip ratio lower bound. That contrast is the basis of the methodological claim.
- The PUSH/PULL contrast becomes more rigorous: "produce based on own inventory state" vs "produce in response to downstream consumption events".
- Service level becomes harsher than under the old ceiling model. PUSH strategies may do relatively better than in prior results, because their over-production cushion now protects against stockouts in a way the old metric did not reward. This shift in relative performance is a finding worth reporting.
- The mass balance assertion is implemented as a new `monitoring/mass_balance.py: MassBalanceMonitor`.
  - **Scheduling.** A SimPy process scheduled at `t = 7, 14, 21, ...` (aligned with weekly consumption ticks), with lower priority than the consumption process. The monitor therefore fires *after* all consumption events at that timestep have settled, which avoids transient violations from intermediate handoff states. The API exposes `check_continuous()` (the scheduled hook) and `check_final()` (end of run, callable from test harnesses without scheduling).
  - **Failure behavior.** An explicit `raise MassBalanceViolation(...)` with a custom exception class, NOT a Python `assert` (which `-O` strips). A flag switches it to warn and continue. The default is raise for development and warn for batch Monte Carlo runs, so one bad seed does not sink 99 good replications. Warnings go to a structured log, so post-hoc analysis can find which seeds drifted. Tolerance is mixed, following `math.isclose`: `abs(delta) <= max(rel_tol * max(abs(lhs), abs(rhs)), abs_tol)` with `rel_tol=1e-6`, `abs_tol=1e-9`. Pure relative tolerance breaks near zero (early in the run before generation begins, or for waste types inactive in some scenarios).
  - **Construction.** The monitor takes an `EntityRegistry` dataclass (`state`, `operators`, `generators`, `collectors`, `transport`) at construction instead of positional args. Tests can mock the registry, and new entity types need no constructor change.
  - **Counters are first-class state on SimulationState.** `processing_losses[W]` is an explicit counter incremented where the loss happens (`_process_waste_transformation`: `processing_losses[W] += (1 - efficiency) × input_amount`). It is NOT derived from yield × processed at check time. Derived quantities accumulate rounding error differently from direct counters, and the invariant would drift for reasons unrelated to bugs.
  - **Telemetry on every check, not just on violation.** `check_continuous()` always writes `(t, lhs, rhs, delta)` to a time-series file. Float drift over a 365-day run is often visible long before it crosses the tolerance, and the time series catches it early.
  - **Tests are required and are the highest-value part of the feature.** At least two synthetic unit tests in `tests/monitoring/test_mass_balance.py`:
    (1) build a registry and run a fake consumption event that violates the invariant (e.g., decrement `finished_goods` without incrementing `consumed`), and confirm `MassBalanceViolation` is raised;
    (2) the same setup with a valid event, and confirm nothing is raised. An untested invariant is worse than none because it gives false confidence. Budget 30 minutes.
  - **Scope phasing: products in Phase A, waste deferred to a P2 follow-up.** Product invariant: `initial_finished_goods[P] + cumulative_produced[P] == cumulative_consumed[P] + current_finished_goods[P] + cumulative_discarded[P]` (`production_discarded` should be `0` by construction under partial-batch). There are no transit complications, since products live only in `finished_goods`. The waste invariant needs non-trivial transit accounting (see the next entry) and is deferred to a follow-up phase to keep Phase A small. The monitor structure (`MassBalanceViolation`, `EntityRegistry`, `check_continuous`/`check_final`, tolerance, telemetry, tests) can be reused when the waste invariant lands.
- Transit accounting (deferred for waste-side mass balance, P2):
  - **Cross-region (collector to collector) transit is real and observable.** `collector.transfer_waste_to_region` (`core/collector.py:500`) decrements source storage immediately, and `CollectorCompany._handle_completed_transport` (`core/collector.py`, inlined from the former `core/collector_utils.py` in Candidate 1) credits the destination on arrival. Between dispatch and arrival the volume sits in `collector.active_transports[].volume`. Delivery has two phases. Cross-region transport delivers waste to the destination *collector*, not directly to the requesting treatment operator, and treatment then pulls locally. Mass balance can read this state by summing `active_transports` across all collectors.
  - **Local pickup (generator to collector via vehicle dispatch) is real but not centrally observable.** `_dispatch_vehicle_for_collection` (`core/collector.py:143`) yields `env.timeout(travel_time)` twice (outbound and return). Between `_perform_collection_at_site` debiting the generator and `_add_to_collection_center` crediting the collector, the waste is in the vehicle, and no aggregated buffer records it. Each vehicle has a scalar `current_load`, not a per-waste-type one, and there is no system-wide iterator over vehicles in flight. The waste invariant needs (a) a per-waste-type `Vehicle.current_load` and (b) an iterator over outbound vehicles. Both are real refactor work outside Phase A scope.
- Two service-level metrics come from the consumption event log. `full_service_level = total_consumed / total_attempted` is the headline and includes both `no_capability` and `stockout` lost sales. `operational_service_level = total_consumed / (total_attempted - no_capability_lost)` is a diagnostic of policy performance on demand the system can operationally fulfill. The same accounting (event log plus reason tags) computes both, and filtering gives both per operator and per product. `full_service_level` is the primary cross-policy comparator, because the `no_capability` floor is identical across all six PUSH/PULL × strategy configurations. `operational_service_level` is the within-feasibility diagnostic. Rejected: a single harsh metric (hides the split between operational and structural gaps that reason tagging was added to expose), and feasible-only (invites reviewer objections about choosing a convenient definition).
- PULL production trigger and once-per-tick idempotency (decided before Phase E, with market-signal granularity settled at the same time). The market-to-treatment notification is a single per-tick, per-operator **Market Signal** (`source_type="market"`, `source_id` = operator name, `volume` = the operator's total producible `attempted`), NOT a per-product payload. Per-product demand lives in the `consumption_events` log. The signal is an edge trigger consumed exactly once. Each pull cycle, the operator scans unacknowledged market signals whose `source_id` is its own name, reads the Consumption Events matching `(operator, signal.timestamp)` from the log, sets each product's production target to that event's `attempted`, and acknowledges the signal. Idempotency reuses `KanbanManager.acknowledged_signals`, with no new per-event state. Correlation is exact. Consumption ticks land on integer multiples of `CONSUMPTION_INTERVAL_DAYS` and the signal shares one timestamp with its events, so float equality on the timestamp is safe. Missed ticks (operator `FAILED` beyond the 24-day signal TTL) expire silently instead of producing a catch-up burst. That is correct, since **Lost Sales** do not backlog. Rejected: a `processed` flag on each event (couples the service-level and mass-balance log to production control, and produces a wrong make-up burst on recovery), and embedding the per-product event payload in the signal (puts back into the signal the data that the aggregate-trigger decision keeps in the log).
- PULL production is event-triggered only (Phase E). For PULL operators, `run_facility` no longer processes waste on its own (`_process_available_waste` becomes PUSH-only). Production fires when the operator's loop next wakes and finds its own unacknowledged **Market Signals**. Per product, the target is the summed `attempted` across those signals' Consumption Events, clamped by `min(attempted/efficiency, waste_storage[input], processing_capacity, finished_goods_headroom/efficiency)`. PUSH keeps the autonomous loop. Production then tracks demand, not waste supply, which keeps the bullwhip contrast `var(production) ≈ var(attempted)` intact and the PUSH/PULL distinction clear. Rejected: (c) keep autonomous processing for PULL. It processes all available waste up to `processing_capacity`, about 45x a cycle's demand (see the capacity note), which saturates finished goods and makes production a function of supply, so PULL would behave as PUSH. Also rejected: (b) a per-product production *budget* drawn down across cycles. The cadence-contingency note explains why it is equivalent to (a) under current parameters but not in general.
  - **Capacity is non-binding by construction.** Per-cycle demand / `processing_capacity` is identical for every operator (about 2.6% at the seasonal peak), because `market_share ∝ waste_storage_capacity` and `processing_capacity = 0.8 × waste_storage_capacity`, so capacity cancels. The production limits that actually bind are waste availability and finished-goods headroom, never `processing_capacity`. If either proportionality breaks (e.g., per-operator `market_share` decoupled from capacity, or a non-uniform `processing_capacity` factor), re-check this, since capacity could start to bind for high-share, low-capacity operators.
  - **(a)≈(b) is contingent, not permanent.** The treatment loop wakes every `processing_time` (24 days) while consumption ticks every `CONSUMPTION_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7). The loop is *coarser* than demand arrival, so about 3 ticks batch into one production cycle, and a budget has no sub-tick cycles to spread across. Option (b) therefore reduces to (a) plus bookkeeping. If `processing_time` is ever set below the consumption interval, the loop would wake more often than demand arrives, and (b)'s budget spreading would start to matter. Revisit the choice then.
  - **Cadence caveat for the bullwhip claim.** Because the loop is coarser than the tick, PULL-ON_DEMAND is lot-for-lot at the *processing* cadence, not the *consumption* cadence. Production batches in lots of about 3 weeks instead of reacting weekly. `var(production) ≈ var(attempted)` still holds under time aggregation, so the "PULL tracks demand" claim holds, but PULL does not respond weekly. If showing *responsiveness* (not just demand tracking) becomes a goal, move `processing_time` toward the consumption interval.
- Phase-C implementation notes (landed 2026-06-01). Implementation settled four choices the design above left open. (1) **Capacity and primed inventory cover only producible products.** `finished_goods_capacity` and the 50% initial inventory are set per output type the operator can make. Non-producible products stay at zero, because the market records them as `no_capability` and never touches their inventory, so priming them would create dead stock. (2) **`blended_efficiency` is the simple mean of `conversion_efficiency`** across the operator's transformations. It only shapes the warm-up waste mix, which collection corrects within about 2 weeks, so precision does not matter. (3) **The waste-priming split uses volume.** `convert_generation_rates_to_volume` converts region generation rates to m³ before computing proportions, so the primed mix matches how waste actually arrives (waste storage is in m³). (4) **The per-output finished-goods headroom clamp moved forward from Phase D**, because making `ProductStorage.capacity` per product forced a change to the old scalar overflow clamp anyway. Production is now clamped by `amount_to_process = min(waste_storage[input], processing_capacity, headroom/efficiency)` with `headroom = finished_goods_capacity[output] - finished_goods[output]`. That allowed deleting the incoherent `product_storage` secondary buffer with no silent product discard. The rest of Phase D (the `_push_collection_logic` rewrite, `S = waste_storage_capacity`, detaching collection from `unmet_demands`) is still deferred. Effect on the headline metric: priming initial inventory raised `full_service_level` from the empty-shelf Phase-B baseline (about 14%) to about 72-80% across the six configurations, with `no_capability_lost = 0` (every operator produces all three products).
- Demand component of transformation prioritization (resolves Q8, decided before Phases D/E). `_get_prioritized_transformations` currently scores each transformation as `abc_priority*2 + demand_score + efficiency + input_availability`, where `demand_score = min(unmet_demands[output]/5000, 1.0)` reads the now-deleted ceiling. **Decision: replace `demand_score` with a finished-goods shortfall term.** The output type whose finished-goods inventory is most depleted relative to its buffer scores highest, so production goes to what most needs restocking. Concretely, `demand_score(output) = max(0, finished_goods_capacity - finished_goods[output]) / finished_goods_capacity`, bounded to `[0, 1]`. (The `finished_goods_capacity` scalar and per-output `finished_goods` levels come from the Phase-C inventory model, and the exact normalization is finalized with the Phase-C capacity calibration.) It is the same for PUSH and PULL, since both prioritize the most-depleted product, and it fits the new model, where production restocks an inventory instead of chasing a cumulative ceiling. The `abc_priority*2` multiplier, efficiency and input-availability terms are unchanged. It is implemented in Phase F when `unmet_demands` is deleted. Rejected: (A) drop the demand component entirely, which loses "what's most needed" steering and leaves only ABC, efficiency and input availability; (C) a rolling-window consumption rate from the event log, which adds a window-length parameter and mixes a rate with level-based terms for no clear gain now (revisit if shortfall steering proves too short-sighted); (D) separate PUSH/PULL scoring paths, which is premature, since the shortfall metric is already the same for both policies.
